### 3. DRAFTED PARAGRAPH

A writing environment equipped with background fact-checking and explicit approval prompts can keep writers closer to shared oversight than full text generation, yet it remains vulnerable to passive deference and stylistic flattening. Within Nandita Biswas Mellamphy's in, on, and out of the loop framework, this interactive workflow positions authors and machines as "co-producers under specific but changing environmental conditions that are conceptualized as being both within and beyond human control" (Mellamphy 2021: 20). Flagging factual errors and requiring accept or deny decisions resembles a coding permission prompt that preserves incremental agency, although Mellamphy notes that practical boundaries between loop positions rarely stay clean. Matt Carlson sharpens this tension by asking "whether an increase in algorithmic judgment will lead to a decline in the authority of human judgment" (Carlson 2015: 429). Relying on an AI editor to straighten unorganized drafts risks imposing generic cadence, while preserving raw wording invites critique over polish. Luke Munn similarly counters narratives of total displacement, yet he warns that people inside partially automated workflows "internalize the logic of the system and perform their activities in a way that is algorithmically recognized" (Munn 2022: 25). Repeated edit prompts can therefore nudge writers toward reflexive approval and machine-friendly prose. Ultimately, background verification and review checkpoints sustain incremental participation while demanding continuous critical scrutiny to prevent quiet surrender of authorship.
