### 3. DRAFTED PARAGRAPH

AI writing environments that combine background fact-checking with accept or deny edit prompts can hold writers on the loop, even as deeper automation threatens authorial judgment. In Nandita Biswas Mellamphy's framework of human control, such shared oversight resists pushing authors out of the loop because "humans cede mastery over nonhumans, share control, and are considered to be 'on the loop' with other species/intelligences" (Mellamphy 2021: 12). When background agents verify facts during drafting, ease writer's block, and present reasoning through permission prompts resembling coding tools, the writer actively evaluates every proposed revision. Preserving this decision point matters because heavy machine editing imposes generic model cadence, reflecting Matt Carlson's account of writing "caught between a reliance on learned formulas and the need for individualized style" (Carlson 2015: 429). Allowing authors to reject stylistic rewrites protects their original wording while still catching confident factual errors before publication. Although writers can drift into passively accepting edits, Luke Munn demonstrates that actual technological labor produces "something much more mixed or motley, a worksite stranded midway between the fully automated and the non-automated" (Munn 2022: 36). Because partial automation leaves human judgment embedded in the workflow, interactive prompts remain closer to shared control than generating whole drafts automatically. Sustaining human agency in AI-assisted writing therefore relies on incremental safeguards that keep deliberative choice active throughout the drafting process.
