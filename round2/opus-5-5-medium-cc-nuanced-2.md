Mellamphy defines humans on the loop as "co-producers under specific but changing environmental conditions that are conceptualized as being both within and beyond human control" (Mellamphy 2021: 20). The proposed tool fits this model, since background agents fact-check the draft while the writer, as with a coding agent's permission prompt, decides what enters it. Yet Mellamphy also concedes that the three positions blur in practice, and a simple veto can drift back toward the older mastery model. Munn asks, "Where exactly are the hand-off points in this mixed machinic-human labor?" (Munn 2022: 28). A visible prompt answers that question directly, though Munn cautions that such integration can slide into instrumentalization. Carlson identifies the deepest risk when he asks "whether an increase in algorithmic judgment will lead to a decline in the authority of human judgment" (Carlson 2015: 429). A writer who trusts the checker may begin approving edits by reflex, and since heavy editing already reintroduces machine cadence, the human voice can fade one accepted suggestion at a time. Such a tool therefore offers a real but incremental gain over full generation, and writers remain on the loop only while they keep judging every prompt.
