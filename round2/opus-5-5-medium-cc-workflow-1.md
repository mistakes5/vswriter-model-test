### 3. Drafted paragraph

AI writing tools can push writers out of the loop, yet a carefully designed writing environment can hold them on it through steady and visible acts of judgment. A writer who drafts freely and then lets an AI editor straighten the result risks giving up both voice and accuracy, since heavy editing reintroduces machine cadence and a confident error can survive an entire draft. Mellamphy's connectionist model offers a stronger arrangement, because "favoring co-production does not entail ceding human oversight altogether" (Mellamphy 2021: 21). In other words, the writer and the system can share the labor of composition while the final decision remains human. Carlson identifies the cost when that decision lapses, observing that "journalistic writing is standardized to exclude individual voice" (Carlson 2015: 425). Automated prose drifts toward the generic, so preserving the writer's own wording becomes a defense of authorship itself. Background agents that flag errors, explain their reasoning, and wait for an accept or deny response work much like the permission prompt of a coding agent. Munn observes this pattern across automated workplaces, describing "a renewed push to actively integrate human labor into automated processes" (Munn 2022: 23). Human participation, on this account, is built into automated systems by design. Writers may still slide into accepting every suggestion, although each prompt keeps a moment of refusal open. Incremental safeguards of this kind therefore hold the writer on the loop far more securely than wholesale generation ever could.
