A writing environment equipped with background fact-checking and explicit accept or deny revision prompts can hold writers on the loop, although maintaining that boundary requires constant vigilance against stylistic homogenization and passive approval. Within political analyses of algorithmic governance, Nandita Biswas Mellamphy separates direct in-the-loop control from full out-of-the-loop automation through "human-on-the-loop" oversight (Biswas Mellamphy 2021: 14). In this configuration, human actors monitor automated processes and retain authority to intervene, which mirrors the writer's comparison to coding permission prompts that flag factual errors, explain their reasoning, and await approval or rejection. By contrast, delegating full drafting or heavy restructuring pushes writers out of the loop by reproducing what Matt Carlson describes as "algorithmic processes that convert data into narrative news texts with limited to no human intervention beyond the initial programming" (Carlson 2015: 417). As the writer notes, intensive machine editing reintroduces a uniform synthetic cadence, creating tension between preserving original wording and accepting structural polish. Even interactive prompts risk drifting toward automatic acceptance, illustrating Luke Munn's argument that "automation is never complete" (Munn 2022: 4). Because human agency and machine assistance remain entangled in practice, maintaining oversight becomes an incremental effort that still retains greater control than full generation. Ultimately, transparent verification tools offer a viable intermediate architecture that keeps human judgment anchored within the writing process.
