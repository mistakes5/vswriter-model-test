You could open by contrasting the pull of full-text generation with a drafting workflow that keeps you actively reviewing background fact-checks and rationale-backed edit prompts. From there, you can position your own choice to preserve original wording against standardized machine cadence as an incremental effort to hold the line on human agency, even when perfect control remains elusive.

### 3. DRAFTED PARAGRAPH

A writing environment equipped with background fact-checking and explicit edit review can hold human writers on the loop, even though generative tools continually pull composition toward full automation. Within Nandita Biswas Mellamphy’s tripartite framework of humans in, on, or out of the loop, shared agency casts participants as "co-producers under specific but changing environmental conditions that are conceptualized as being both within and beyond human control" (Mellamphy 2021: 20). Background agents that verify claims during drafting, ease writer’s block, and display rationale-backed accept or deny prompts act like coding permission checks that preserve deliberative judgment. Yet heavy machine editing threatens expressive autonomy, because Matt Carlson notes that automated writing leaves composition "caught between a reliance on learned formulas and the need for individualized style" (Carlson 2015: 429). When algorithmic revision overwrites rough drafts, standardized machine cadence displaces personal wording, making deliberate retention of original phrasing vital despite occasional critiques. Even with review prompts, writers risk drifting into passive auto-acceptance, echoing Luke Munn’s finding that contemporary workers "operate at the fuzzy nexus of partial automation" (Munn 2022: 36). Partial oversight remains fragile as cognitive fatigue erodes active scrutiny, yet evaluating flagged edits retains greater agency than delegating entire drafts. Holding writers on the loop therefore depends on incremental design choices that keep human judgment visible at every editorial threshold.
