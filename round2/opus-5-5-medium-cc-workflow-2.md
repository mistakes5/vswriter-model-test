**3. DRAFTED PARAGRAPH**

AI writing tools threaten to push writers out of the loop whenever they generate or heavily rewrite prose, yet a writing environment built around background fact-checking and accept/deny edit suggestions can keep the writer on the loop. Mellamphy describes the on-the-loop position as one in which humans act as "co-producers under specific but changing environmental conditions that are conceptualized as being both within and beyond human control" (Mellamphy 2021: 20). In such an environment, background agents check claims as the writer drafts, so part of the process runs beyond the writer's control, but the final decision on every flagged edit stays with the writer. Carlson raises the question of "whether an increase in algorithmic judgment will lead to a decline in the authority of human judgment" (Carlson 2015: 429). This danger is real for writers, since heavy AI editing reintroduces a generic machine cadence and replaces the writer's voice with that of the tool. Munn asks, "Where exactly are the hand-off points in this mixed machinic-human labor?" (Munn 2022: 28). A permission prompt answers this question by making each hand-off visible, because the writer can read the reasoning behind a correction before accepting or denying it. Writers may still drift into accepting suggestions by habit, although this partial control keeps them far more engaged than wholesale generation does. Writers may hold the line on authorship only in increments, and each decision they keep strengthens their claim to the work.
