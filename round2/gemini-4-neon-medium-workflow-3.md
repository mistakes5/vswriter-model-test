### 3. DRAFTED PARAGRAPH

AI writing environments with background fact-checking and explicit approval prompts can hold writers on the loop incrementally, even though heavy editing and passive acceptance continually strain human agency. In Nandita Biswas Mellamphy's framework of humans in, on, or out of the loop, this collaborative design reflects a scenario where "humans cede mastery over nonhumans, share control, and are considered to be 'on the loop' with other species/intelligences" (Mellamphy 2021: 12). When background agents verify factual claims during drafting, prevent writer's block, and present revisions through permission prompts explaining errors, the writer shares labor with software while deciding whether to accept or deny each change. Using an automated editor to organize scattered thoughts still creates stylistic tension, as Matt Carlson notes that automated writing "forces a reexamination of newswriting as caught between a reliance on learned formulas and the need for individualized style" (Carlson 2015: 429). Heavy rewriting smooths rough prose while reintroducing generic phrasing, whereas preserving personal wording protects voice while inviting critique. Repeated prompts also risk inducing automatic approval, echoing Luke Munn's observation that mediated workers "internalize the logic of the system and perform their activities in a way that is algorithmically recognized" (Munn 2022: 25). Over time, reflexive acceptance can reshape authorial judgment toward algorithmic defaults. Although perfect control remains elusive, incremental review mechanisms sustain human oversight far better than surrendering entire drafts to full machine generation.
