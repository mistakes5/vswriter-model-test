**3. DRAFTED PARAGRAPH**

A writing environment that checks facts in the background and asks the writer to accept or deny each proposed edit can hold writers on the loop, although passive acceptance remains a risk. Mellamphy describes humans on the loop as "co-producers under specific but changing environmental conditions that are conceptualized as being both within and beyond human control" (Mellamphy 2021: 20). In such a tool, the writer pours out unorganized ideas while background agents verify claims and offer strategies against writer's block, so authorship becomes shared while the final decision stays human. Full generation carries a cost that Carlson identifies in automated news, which journalists judged to be "passably human but entirely generic" (Carlson 2015: 424). Heavy machine editing brings this flat cadence into student prose, which explains why a writer might preserve their own wording despite critique. Munn asks, "Where exactly are the hand-off points in this mixed machinic-human labor?" (Munn 2022: 28). A permission prompt answers this question by turning every flagged error into a visible hand-off, where the writer reads the reasoning and then chooses to accept or deny the change. Writers may still drift into approving every prompt, yet this arrangement keeps far more judgment with them than a tool that generates the entire text. Holding the line may remain unfinished work, and each decision returned to the writer is a real step toward it.
