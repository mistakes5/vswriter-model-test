### 3. DRAFTED PARAGRAPH

An integrated writing environment can keep authors actively engaged with artificial intelligence, yet it exposes a persistent tension between structural coherence and individual voice. Traditional software development environments scaffolded human labor without replacing it, and a writing environment built around active brainstorming and guided reading similarly positions writers and software as "co-producers under specific but changing environmental conditions that are conceptualized as being both within and beyond human control" (Mellamphy 2021: 20). Within this shared loop, the writer must still execute the core intellectual work while the interface surfaces relevant texts and prompts initial momentum. Even when the writer remains on the loop, treating artificial intelligence as a book editor who straightens out unorganized drafts raises a critical design problem, as Luke Munn asks, "Where exactly are the hand-off points in this mixed machinic-human labor?" (Munn 2022: 28). Determining where human drafting ends and automated revision begins directly shapes who governs the final prose. Heavy editorial intervention risks reintroducing predictable machine cadence, echoing how Matt Carlson observes that "journalists consistently judged the quality of its automated stories to be passably human but entirely generic" (Carlson 2015: 424). Conversely, restricting the system to preserve raw phrasing protects authenticity while leaving rough transitions open to critique. Sustaining human authorship in an assisted writing environment therefore depends on calibrating these editorial boundaries so that scaffolding strengthens human expression without flattening it into formulaic prose.
