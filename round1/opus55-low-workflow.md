## 3. Drafted Paragraph (~225 words)

An IDE-style writing environment can hold writers on the loop when it treats AI as an editor of scattered ideas and keeps the writer's own wording at the center of revision. Mellamphy describes humans and nonhumans as "co-producers under specific but changing environmental conditions that are conceptualized as being both within and beyond human control" (Mellamphy 2021: 20). This model fits the brainstorming stage, where a writer releases unorganized ideas and the tool helps link them, much as an editor shapes a book manuscript. Yet this editor carries a cost. Carlson reports that automated news stories were judged "passably human but entirely generic" (Carlson 2015: 424). The same risk follows heavy AI editing, since each additional pass reintroduces a recognizable LLM cadence that erases individual voice. Preserving raw wording reduces that cadence, although it invites critiques of unpolished prose. Munn insists that "full automation itself was always a fantasy, a vision decoupled from the complexities and contingencies of reality" (Munn 2022: 19). His claim explains why the writer must remain the one who drafts, because the contingent judgments that give a text its voice cannot be handed to a machine. Traditional IDEs offered open references, bookmarks, and prompts while leaving the code to the programmer, and a writing IDE can do the same by guiding writers toward the page without composing it for them.
