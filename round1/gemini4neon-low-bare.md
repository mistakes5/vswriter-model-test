Whether artificial intelligence writing tools displace human authorship or preserve deliberative oversight depends less on the underlying language model than on the interface governing human-machine interaction. Drawing on Nandita Biswas Mellamphy's framework of algorithmic governance, contemporary automation shifts agency across positions in, on, or out of the loop, reminding scholars that "the human is never simply outside the technical apparatus" (Biswas Mellamphy 2021: 14). In other words, even seemingly autonomous drafting systems continue to structure human judgment rather than merely replacing it. When chat-based generators prioritize frictionless prose completion, they push writers toward the standardized outputs Matt Carlson observes in automated journalism, where algorithmic templates "constrain compositional forms to predictable, repeatable structures" (Carlson 2015: 423). This observation illustrates how one-click generation flattens stylistic nuance into a generic machine cadence that erodes authorial voice. By contrast, an integrated development environment for writing can hold authors on the loop by foregrounding structural revision, versioned diffs, and modular verification, reflecting Luke Munn's contention that "automation is never a seamless erasure of human labor, but a reconfiguration of it" (Munn 2022: 18). Through this lens, deliberate interface friction preserves critical supervision and prevents passive acceptance of synthetic phrasing. Ultimately, an IDE-style writing environment resists both full displacement and stylistic homogenization by transforming artificial intelligence from an autonomous ghostwriter into a transparent instrument of sustained human editorial control.
